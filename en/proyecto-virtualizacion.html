01

Executive summary

Virtualisation consolidates resources, isolates incompatible applications, simplifies administration and improves availability in a research institute where applications and systems have different technical requirements. The case compares VMware ESXi/vCenter and Microsoft Hyper-V as mature alternatives for corporate and scientific environments.

02

VMware ESXi + vCenter

ESXi

A type 1 hypervisor for running isolated virtual machines and managing CPU, memory, networking and storage.

vCenter

Centralised management of hosts, VMs, clusters, resources and operational policies.

Mobility

vMotion and Storage vMotion enable live migration of workloads and storage, depending on the edition and configuration.

Continuity

vSphere HA and DRS provide failure recovery and resource distribution within the cluster.

03

Hyper-V

The Microsoft alternative provides resource consolidation, support for different guest systems, high availability and migration mechanisms, plus integration with Windows Server, PowerShell and centralised administration tools.

04

Compatibility and performance

The coexistence of Windows and Linux does not prevent the project, but shapes the delivery model and technology chosen. Applications with complex graphics or intensive processing require appropriate CPU, RAM, GPU, storage and network sizing; feasibility should not be confused with straightforward implementation.

05

Services and storage

RDS

Centralises desktops and applications, simplifying patching, security and remote access, although it does not eliminate incompatibilities or specialised hardware needs.

iSCSI

Encapsulates SCSI storage traffic over IP networks. The design proposes a dedicated network/VLAN, access control and redundant paths where appropriate.

Hyper-V drivers

Synthetic devices using VMBus are the preferred option; legacy/emulated devices are reserved for compatibility.

Changes and backup

Snapshots facilitate operational rollback before sensitive changes, but do not replace an independent backup.

06

Optimisation and validation

The case proposes reducing unnecessary activity, prioritising synthetic adapters, reviewing background services and tasks, allocating vCPUs carefully and validating migration/HA, scientific workloads, Windows/Linux access and restoration from an independent backup before production.

CONCLUSION

Choose the platform for its suitability.

VMware stands out for its vSphere ecosystem and centralised management; Hyper-V for its integration with Microsoft environments. The final decision depends on applications, criticality, compatibility, licensing, performance and the actual user profile.
